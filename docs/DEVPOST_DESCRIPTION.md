Ask the Sheet: iNViSU reads the Library of Congress drawings

The Library of Congress holds about 55,000 public-domain documentation sets of American buildings: the Historic American Buildings Survey and its engineering and landscape siblings. Hand-lettered measured drawings scanned at 400 dpi, survey photographs, typed data pages. They are the primary record of what the country built, and not one of them can be asked a question. The drawings have no text layer, the lettering is by hand, and the cross-references are words on the sheet ("SEE SHEET 12"), not links. Preservation architects, historians and the people who maintain these buildings read them the way they were read in 1964: sheet by sheet, by eye.

This entry makes one of them answerable. Pick Iolani Palace, Honolulu (HABS HI-1, loc.gov hi0047): 85 pages, 23 scanned drawing sheets from the 1964-66 survey, 57 photographs. Ask "where is the throne room" and you get ANSWERED, with the plan sheet as a clickable receipt and the lettering it relied on boxed on the drawing. Ask "which sheets show the Richards Street elevation" and you get the sheets. Ask "what does HABS sheet 12 reference" and the typed cross-reference lettering becomes the receipt. Ask something the set does not say and you get NOT IN THE SET, with the pages it checked. Exactly four outcomes are allowed: answered with receipts, answered from memory with its date, not in the set, unknown entity. Receipts are verified in code against the index; a cited page that does not exist is flagged, never shown as fact. Numbers, areas, dates and sheet numbers are never estimated.

How it is built. Every model is an NVIDIA Nemotron open model and every call goes to Nebius through one OpenAI-compatible client. It is a chained, unattended workflow with no human between the loc.gov download and the answered question: ingest, full-sheet read, quadrant re-read, dedupe, typed "SEE SHEET N" references mapped through each sheet's own "SHEET N OF 22" lettering, a SQLite index that also records every reference whose target is missing, an evidence pack per question (inventory, typed links, the cross-reference check, term hits, then the full text of every page under a budget, anything dropped named), the brain's answer in one of the four outcomes, code-side receipt verification, and a second call if the model skips its outcome line.

What ran. Nano for the everyday reads, Ultra for the serious reasoning, the tiering the track describes, on both Nebius surfaces with the same client. The eyes: nvidia/Nemotron-3-Nano-Omni-30B-A3B-Reasoning-FP8 on Nebius AI Cloud Serverless AI, one endpoint, one NVIDIA L40S, the stock vLLM image (NVIDIA's card names a single L40S as that build's minimum GPU; it is). 23 of 23 sheets, 1,818 text entries, 23 titles, 47 typed cross-references, 7 unresolved (all a sheet 22 this LOC record lacks), 124 minutes of a preemptible L40S, about $1.59. The brain in the video: nvidia/Nemotron-3-Ultra-550b-a55b on Nebius Token Factory, median 4.7 seconds per 30,000-token question, reasoning returned separately, every receipt verified. Before the credits landed, Omni answered the same four questions from the same endpoint; both sets of transcripts are committed. Nemotron-3.5-Super-VL also read the same 23 sheets on Token Factory, scored on the same metric and committed: 1,779 entries, 19 of 23 self-numbers, 37 reference letterings, 28 of 117 calls at the token ceiling while reasoning, against Omni's 1,818, 22 and 47 with every call finished. One set, one resolution, two settings: a measurement, not a ranking.

What we had to code around, found by reading the model's output rather than the logs: a repetition loop at temperature 0 (repetition_penalty 1.05 and a loop guard), a bare JSON array instead of the requested object on the quadrant prompt (the first parser dropped all 92 replies and reported "+0", a zero that had to be read), boxes mixing pixel and fraction scales, inline reasoning when vLLM has no reasoning parser, and a 30B brain that answered the reverse direction of a reference question until the evidence pack named incoming versus outgoing links.

Limits, stated plainly. One structure in the demo set; the pipeline is generic, the evidence is not yet. The public Space is index search over exported JSON and never calls a model. Small lettering can be missed at 1,400 px; quadrant re-reads produce cut words at tile edges. Box precision is enough to point at lettering, not to measure from. A verified page is not a verified reading. English only. Not built for accessibility.

What's next. The same steps work on any HABS/HAER/HALS record; a batch runner over loc.gov record IDs lets a preservation office point at its county and get every set answerable. The eyes get trained: Nemotron-Nano-V2-12B VL is fine-tunable on Token Factory, and we have a 228,000-box self-labeled corpus of drawing text with exact positions built for exactly that. Then iNViSU Spatial Memory verified by MAG, so answers a human confirmed are remembered across sets. Open method on open models: MIT on GitHub, documents public domain from loc.gov.
