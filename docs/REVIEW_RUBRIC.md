# The organizers' rubric, as they wrote it

Quoted from the hackathon site and the organizer emails received by the registrant
(October 2026). `tools/self_review.py` scores this entry against these texts with the
sponsors' own models (Nemotron 3 Ultra and Nemotron 3.5 Super-VL on Nebius Token
Factory); the full saved pages and emails it reads are kept locally, not in the repo.

## How judging works

> Submissions first go through a pass/fail check for baseline viability and fit with
> the theme. Everything that passes is then scored 1–5 on four equally weighted
> criteria: Technological Implementation (how effectively you use Nebius Token
> Factory/AI Cloud and the NVIDIA model), Design, Potential Impact, and Quality of
> the Idea.

## The track

> Best Apps and Agents Track. Build any app or agent someone would actually use, from
> a productivity tool or copilot to a workflow that runs itself. Power it with
> Nemotron models on Nebius through Token Factory. Reach for Nemotron 3 Ultra when you
> need serious reasoning, and let Nano or Super handle the fast, everyday calls, so
> your app stays responsive and your credits stretch further. We encourage you to
> deploy your application with Nebius Serverless Endpoints, or use Nebius Serverless
> Jobs for background processing and asynchronous workflows, though neither is
> required.

> Best Apps and Agents: a single API call demo works; a multi-step autonomous
> workflow that chains tools end to end stands out more.

## Submission requirements (from the organizers' checklist)

Runs on Nebius Token Factory or AI Cloud and uses at least one NVIDIA open model;
fits one track; demo video under 3 minutes, public on YouTube, showing the app
working and explaining the Nebius and NVIDIA usage; public repository with a visible
open-source license and a README with setup and execution instructions; a working
demo URL; the feedback form on Nebius tools and NVIDIA tech; no edits after the
deadline (Friday 2026-10-30, 10:00 AM PT).

## Tips for a stronger submission

> Name your project like a human, not an algorithm. Make it memorable, and make it
> mean something specific to what you actually built.

> Hide your API keys. Never put your API key directly in your code. Use environment
> variables or a .env file in your .gitignore, and check your repo before you submit.

> Make the required tools impossible to miss. Name Nebius Token Factory/AI Cloud and
> your NVIDIA model in your project description, add them to your Built With section,
> and call them out clearly — with audio — in your demo video, not just a passing
> mention.

> Treat your video like a pitch, not a tutorial. You've got 3 minutes. Lead with the
> problem, show the solution working, and tell judges what problem it solves, who
> it's for, how it uses Nebius and NVIDIA, and what the working app actually does.

## Judges' advice (earlier organizer email)

Deliver the aha within the first 30 seconds; do one thing well; publish your
limitations.
